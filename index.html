  1. Find or add. Search what you already know — people and organizations — before adding, so you never make a duplicate. Type a name; add precision keys (email / LinkedIn) to resolve exactly. Pick a stream or an open task — or paste any text — and Currents ranks the people whose current drivers and needs sit closest to it, showing the signal that made each one rank. Read-only.   3. Ambiguous matches. Same-name twins routed for human disposal (§3.6). Never auto-merged.
  4. Active initiatives. Themes you've dispatched. Each ranked people and minted one fit-thesis per person into your Theses feed — click a row to see those theses.
  5. Declare an initiative. A top-down theme dispatched as individuated fit-hypotheses — never a mail-merge. Currents embeds the intent, ranks people by how their current drivers/needs fit it, and composes one grounded fit-claim per person that rides the normal disposal feed.
  6. Log a note. Raw signal into person_raw_inputs — the fuel person-listener extracts from. Search for the person, or it auto-fills after an add.
  7. Add asset. Your own content — value props, writings, talks. The resonator scores these against world-events; outreach-composer cites them. Not derived from the spine — this is the only way to seed them.
  8. Asset library. Your seeded assets, ordered by the resonator's live relevance score. Click “edit” to revise one (re-embeds on save when title/summary/body change).
  9. Add a news source. The daily sweep list. A source added here is fetched by rss-adapter every four hours, and every story it files carries its name through the News tab and into per-source performance. Sectors and topics are stamped on every story the feed produces, so they are how it gets found later, not decoration. Only rss is swept today. There is no delete: switching a source off on the News tab keeps its past stories attributable, which deleting it would not.

Notes
  1. Pending theses. The disposal feed: each row is one surfaced claim awaiting your answer, and answering is what trains the loop. Accept spawns a task; Done means you already acted and it was right, which counts as a hit and spawns nothing; Reject asks for a reason, and only “wrong” debits calibration — the rest route to eligibility, threshold or matching. Snooze defers without recording an outcome. The disposal rate is the learning rate.
  2. Why · provenance. What the claim rests on: the world-events linked to it, and — for any person subject — the soft-layer grounding behind “◇ see grounding”, which reads their current drivers and needs. Read-only, and raw model confidence is never shown here or anywhere. The band on a claim is empirical, derived from how that generator's past claims resolved.

Notes
  1. Do Next. Your commitments — a task is spawned when you accept or edit a suggestion. Complete / Abandon is the outcome signal the loop grades against; Still live pushes the review date out. Unfiled tasks sit under Unassigned.
  2. Due for review. Past their review date. Each wants one of three answers: Complete (did it), Abandon (dropped it), or Still live (neither — push the date out). Renewing is recorded, so declining to close is a signal rather than silence.
  3. Ranked. One ranked list across every stream, oldest-pressing first. Use “Still live” to push a task's review date out.
  4. Unassigned. Standalone tasks, not tied to any stream. Legitimate for one-off relationship maintenance — or file one into a stream with the picker.

Notes
  1. Streams. Durable workspaces — one per product or multi-stakeholder project. A stream holds its stakeholders, its organizations, its tasks and the theses folded into it. Tasks that belong to no stream sit under Floating tasks in the same column, because filing one is a move up into the list above it.
  2. Outcome: dropped is not ended. Ended means the work concluded with neither a win nor a loss. Dropped means you withdrew before it concluded, and that is the one the withdrawal rate counts. The distinction is the point: dropping the claims you have lost faith in improves apparent accuracy without improving judgement, so the two are never merged.
